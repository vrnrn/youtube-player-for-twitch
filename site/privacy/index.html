Privacy

Privacy Policy

Last updated October 25, 2026

This policy describes the YouTube Player for Twitch Chrome extension, this information site, and the separate workflow used by the maintainer to submit extension releases.

Information stored by the extension

The extension does not require an account. It stores its settings and playback state in Chrome’s extension-local storage on your device. This includes Auto-sync and quality preferences, remembered YouTube video IDs for Twitch channels, and the current active video ID for a channel.

Recent-stream history can include a YouTube video ID, its title and channel name, the time it was added, and whether you pinned it. The history list can be cleared from the extension. The extension’s local storage is cleared when the extension is removed. When you enable Force Highest Quality, the extension also reads and updates Twitch’s local video-quality preference in your browser.

The optional experimental VAFT interruption-blocking preference is also stored locally and defaults to off. While YouTube is active, the extension remembers the original Twitch pause, mute and volume settings for that channel so it can restore them after a page reload or player replacement.

The optional preference to hide Twitch player extensions is stored locally. It hides native player overlays and buttons; it does not change browser extensions or intercept network requests.

Optional floating chat stores its enablement, appearance and position locally. It mirrors recent messages, badges and emotes already displayed in the current Twitch chat. Mirrored messages remain in memory and are not added to extension storage or sent to the developer. The overlay does not open another chat connection; mirrored images can load from their existing HTTPS image URLs. Extra emotes use a separate setting and catalog as described below.

Experimental Kick support

Kick support is off by default. When enabled in Settings, the extension stores your Kick preferences, pinned channel names and active stream substitutions locally. It requests public channel status directly from kick.com while your Twitch tab is visible, without cookies or account credentials. Selected streams load from player.kick.com, and optional Kick chat loads from kick.com in a separate frame. These frames use Kick’s normal browser session and may process ordinary request information, cookies and chat interactions under Kick’s Privacy Policy. The extension does not capture or store Kick chat messages, send them to the developer, or create a Twitch account for a pinned channel. Turning support off removes Kick frames and stops channel checks; saved pins remain on your device until unpinned or the extension is removed.

Requests to YouTube and Twitch

When you search for a stream, the extension reads the current Twitch channel name and, when available, the YouTube channel link shown on that Twitch page. It requests YouTube search results, public channel-stream information, or video-page details as needed. The search term, linked channel URL, and selected video ID may be included in those requests. Playback loads the selected video using YouTube’s embedded player.

These requests go from your browser to YouTube; the extension does not proxy them through a developer-operated server. YouTube may receive ordinary request information and process it under Google’s Privacy Policy and YouTube’s privacy information. Twitch also processes your use of Twitch under its Privacy Notice.

If you enable experimental VAFT interruption blocking, the bundled TwitchAdSolutions algorithm filters Twitch advertising segments and requests alternate Twitch playback tokens and playlists through your browser’s existing Twitch player context. It may use Twitch’s ordinary client, device, session, authorization and integrity headers for requests to Twitch’s GraphQL endpoint; these values are not sent to the developer or stored in extension history. It also handles Twitch media playlists and segments on Twitch’s video-delivery domains. It reads Twitch’s player-worker source in the page context and runs it in a locally created worker with playlist hooks. There is no runtime download of VAFT updates, developer proxy or telemetry. VAFT does not run in the YouTube iframe or on YouTube pages.

To register these optional hooks before Twitch’s player starts, the extension requests Chrome’s scripting permission and host access only to https://www.twitch.tv/*, in addition to its existing YouTube access. Changing the preference reloads Twitch. Other open Twitch tabs need a reload to apply the change.

Extra emotes

Extra emotes are enabled by default and can be turned off under YPFT → Additional settings → Chat. When Twitch chat is open, the extension requests public global and current-channel catalogs directly from BetterTTV (api.betterttv.net), FrankerFaceZ (api.frankerfacez.com) and 7TV (7tv.io). If FrankerFaceZ has no room record, the extension resolves the public channel ID through Twitch (gql.twitch.tv). Emote host permissions are limited to these APIs. Normal chat and floating chat load matching images from those providers' HTTPS CDNs.

Catalog requests omit credentials, chat messages and viewer usernames. Channel requests include the current channel's public login or Twitch ID. The extension stores channel names and IDs, public emote codes, provider names, image URLs and cache expiry times locally. Global catalogs refresh after six hours and channel catalogs after thirty minutes; at most ten recent channels are cached. A provider outage retains its last catalog and schedules a later retry. Chat messages are not stored or sent to these providers. Image requests omit the referrer. Providers can receive ordinary request information, such as your IP address and requested resource, under their own policies.

Sharing by the extension

The extension does not send its locally stored settings, playback state, or stream history to the developer. The current extension source contains no developer analytics, advertising, or telemetry service.

This information site

This static site does not provide accounts or forms. It is hosted by Cloudflare and uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to measure site traffic and page performance. These website metrics are separate from the extension, which does not include analytics. When visitors access the site, Cloudflare may process information such as IP addresses, traffic-routing data, system configuration information, and other information about traffic to and from the site. See Cloudflare’s Privacy Policy.

Your choices

You can clear the extension’s recent-stream history from its menu. Removing the extension clears its extension-local storage. You can also choose whether to use YouTube or Twitch features and review the privacy controls offered by those services.

You can turn VAFT interruption blocking off in the YouTube menu; after the Twitch page reloads, its integrated hooks are removed. If you already use another VAFT userscript, disable that individual script before enabling the integrated option so two installations do not compete. The extension does not change other extensions or userscripts.
